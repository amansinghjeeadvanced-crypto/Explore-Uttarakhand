Jim Corbett National Park: Pioneer Tiger Sanctuary

Where the forested Shivalik foothills meet the meandering Ramganga River. Celebrated for wild Asian elephant herds, over 580 bird species, and world-renowned Royal Bengal tiger conservation.

Overview & Geographical Setting

Established in 1936 as Hailey National Park, Jim Corbett National Park holds the prestigious honor of being mainland Asia's very first national park. Renamed in 1957 in tribute to legendary hunter-turned-conservationist Jim Corbett, this iconic wilderness served as the historic launching ground for India's landmark Project Tiger in April 1973.

Encompassing a core protected expanse of 520.8 square kilometers (within a total tiger reserve landscape of 1,288 square kilometers), Corbett bridges the subtropical sub-Himalayan belt across Nainital, Almora, and Pauri Garhwal districts. The sanctuary's geography is defined by the crystalline Ramganga, Kosi, and Mandal rivers, dense moist deciduous Sal (Shorea robusta) canopies, rugged sandstone ravines, and expansive alluvial grasslands locally termed chaurs.

Wildlife Highlights & Biodiversity

Corbett's intricate mosaic of riverine forests, marshlands, and high-grass savannahs sustains one of the highest densities of wild carnivores and large herbivores in South Asia:

Royal Bengal Tiger (Panthera tigris)

Corbett harbors a thriving wild tiger population. While tigers move freely across extensive home ranges and sightings are never guaranteed, early morning and late afternoon safaris through sal forests and water crossings provide optimal tracking opportunities.

Asian Elephant (Elephas maximus)

Spectacular breeding herds of wild elephants frequent the Ramganga reservoir and Dhikala grasslands. Watch herds forage peacefully in early summer evenings against the backdrop of forested ridges.

Ramganga Aquatic Fauna

The clean waters of the Ramganga River harbor critically endangered fish-eating Gharials, Marsh Mugger crocodiles, and the prized golden Mahseer fish, observable from high riverbank lookouts.

Avian Diversity (580+ Species)

An international birdwatching paradise. Key species include the Great Hornbill, Crested Serpent Eagle, Pallas's Fish Eagle, Collared Falconet, Brown Fish Owl, and dozens of winter Palearctic migratory waterfowl.

Important Note on Wildlife Sightings

Jim Corbett National Park is a completely wild, unconfined natural ecosystem. Animals have extensive territory and roam unhindered. No ethical guide or park authority guarantees wildlife sightings. The true joy of an authentic safari lies in tracking pugmarks, listening for deer and langur alarm calls, and immersing yourself in the raw natural beauty of the Himalayan foothills.

Safari Zones & Official Booking Logistics

To balance wildlife preservation with responsible tourism, Corbett is organized into distinct ecotourism zones, each possessing individual entry gates, terrain characteristics, and visitor capacities:

1. Dhikala Zone (Entry: Dhangarhi Gate)

The most famous and panoramic sector of Corbett, overlooking the vast Patli Dun valley and Ramganga reservoir. Open from November 15 to June 15. Day visitors can access Dhikala via scheduled government Canter safaris. Private open-top jeep safaris inside Dhikala are strictly reserved for guests with confirmed overnight reservations in government Forest Rest Houses (FRHs).

2. Bijrani Zone (Entry: Aamdanda Gate)

Renowned for scenic dry stream beds (sots), dense sal tree stands, and excellent birdwatching. Highly popular for open 4x4 Gypsy day safaris operating in fixed morning and afternoon shifts. Open from October 15 to June 30.

3. Jhirna Zone (Entry: Dhela Gate)

Located in the southern region with mixed bamboo and deciduous woodlands. Open round the year for morning and afternoon day safaris (subject to road safety during intense monsoon spells).

4. Dhela Zone (Entry: Dhela Gate)

Inaugurated in 2014 as an eco-tourism buffer zone. Features open waterbodies and broad grasslands, making it an exceptional zone for birding and herbivore tracking throughout the year.

5. Durga Devi Zone (Entry: Durga Devi Gate)

Nestled in the rugged northeastern hills along the Ramganga and Mandal rivers. A serene sanctuary for wild elephants, otters, and riverine birds. Open from November 15 to June 15.

How to Book Official Safari Permits

All day-safari permits and government Forest Rest House bookings are regulated strictly by the Uttarakhand Forest Department through its official online reservation portal. To protect habitat integrity, vehicle permits are capped per zone and shift. Permits are non-transferable and require valid government photo ID details at the time of booking. Plan your reservations 45 to 90 days in advance, particularly for weekends and peak winter/summer seasons.

Verified Best Time to Visit & Seasonal Guidance

Corbett experiences distinct foothill seasons, from chilly, crisp Himalayan winters to dry, tracking-friendly summer heat:

Season Months Temperature Highlights & Advice
Winter (Prime) November to February 5°C to 24°C All core zones open. Pleasant daytime sun; chilly morning safaris. Superb for migratory birds. Pack heavy windproof thermal jackets, caps, and gloves.
Summer (Wildlife) March to June 19°C to 40°C Deciduous foliage thins, making animals easier to spot near riverbanks and watering points. Carry wide-brim hats, sunscreen, sunglasses, and plentiful drinking water.
Monsoon (Green) July to September 24°C to 32°C Heavy seasonal rains refresh river basins. Core zones (Dhikala, Bijrani, Durga Devi) close for animal breeding and road protection. Jhirna and Dhela remain open weather permitting.

Ethical Safari Code & Mandatory Park Rules

Core Visitor Guidelines for Responsible Safaris

Jim Corbett National Park is a strictly protected wildlife habitat. Violations of park regulations incur severe financial penalties and legal action under the Wildlife Protection Act:

  • Remain Inside Your Vehicle: Never step down from your safari Gypsy or Canter inside the national park, except at designated fenced rest house compounds.
  • Wear Subdued Clothing: Dress in nature-blending earthy tones like khaki, olive green, beige, or brown. Avoid bright neons, pure white, and strong perfumes.
  • Absolute Silence: Keep talking to a quiet whisper. Mobile phones must be switched to silent mode. Do not play car horns, music, or shouted alerts.
  • Zero Littering & Plastic Ban: Single-use plastic bottles, polythene carry bags, and snack wrappers are banned inside the reserve. Pack all waste back to town.
  • Maintain Safe Distance: Never demand that your driver chase or crowd wild animals. Maintain a respectful buffer of at least 20 meters, particularly around elephant herds with calves.
  • No Flash Photography: Flash disorients wild animals and can trigger aggressive defensive charges. Optical zoom lenses and binoculars are strongly recommended.

Practical Travel Tips & Route Guidance

How to Reach Jim Corbett National Park (Base Town: Ramnagar):

  • By Train: The nearest railway terminus is Ramnagar Railway Station (RMR), located 1 to 3 km from forest reception centers. Direct overnight trains run daily from New Delhi (Ranikhet Express and Corbett Park Link Express).
  • By Air: The closest operational airport is Pantnagar Airport (PGH), located approximately 80 km away. New Delhi International Airport (approx. 265 km) offers the widest domestic and international connections with highway transit.
  • By Road: Ramnagar is connected to Delhi via a well-maintained 260 km highway corridor through Hapur, Moradabad, and Kashipur (approx. 5 to 6 hours driving time). Private taxis and state transport buses operate regularly.
